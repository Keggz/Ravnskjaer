Blodskjæft is the name Øystæinn gave for a cave by Myrkagap, a few days' journey northeast of Ravnskjær.

## Øystæinn's account

During the spring feast in early summer 795, he said that creatures dwelling here forced him to obtain trade goods and information about Ravnskjær's leadership and clans. They threatened those close to him. According to him, they also had other contacts.

The company decided Øystæinn would guide them and prepared to leave a few days later. [The account of the journey follows here](/en/timeline/da-jegerne-ble-bytte).

[Read about the revelation during the feast](/en/timeline/varfesten).

## What the company found

Outside, they saw people coming and going with goods from north, east and south. Inside the cave were trade goods, old weapons and traces of blood. Daylight reached only a short distance, and the passages continued far beyond the chamber Øystæinn knew.

On the right beyond the entrance lay a dark pool with bones at its edge and red trails in the water. Farther in they found a closed, carved stone coffin they left untouched, and three sleeping places with hides, urns and barrels.

A round stone altar marked with symbols along its edge stood where several passages met. A cut-open corpse lay beside it, and blood flowed onward along channels through the cave. A narrow passage led towards a larger chamber with benches, weapon racks and shields. Here the company was divided and attacked from several sides.

## The battle and return

Three vampires were beheaded. Øystæinn died, while the other six survived with life-threatening injuries. Saia lost an eye and suffered a severe facial wound.

They found trade goods, silver, metals, weapons, armour and shields, but took only what they could carry. A couple of coins bore a mark they did not recognise. Øystæinn's body was brought home, along with the vampires' bodies and severed heads. The vampires' remains were shown only to the small council.

After their return, Æthelberth linked the mark on the coins to Alcuin, called “Alcuin the Great”, a scholar at Charlemagne's court. He read the find as a trace of the night creatures' connections southward and warned of powers weakening communities ahead of Christian conversion. [His explanation is recorded in the next chronicle](/en/timeline/ferden-som-stanset-ved-skogkanten).
