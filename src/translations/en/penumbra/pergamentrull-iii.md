This is the text Halvdan brought to Trym during [the spring feast in 795](/en/timeline/varfesten). It is attributed to Brother Matthaeus of Lindisfarne. Its advice records the author's experiences and beliefs; it does not establish that every night creature follows the same rules.

---

*Attributed to Brother Matthaeus of Lindisfarne, written in fear and sleepless nights.*

I write now not as a man safe in God's house, but as one who has seen something the world cannot contain in words.

For there are dead who do not rest in the Lord, nor decay as other dead do. They rise, but not to salvation. They walk, but not among the living as equals. They bear a face, a voice and memory, and these are their first weapons.

Many believe a monster will always reveal itself as a monster. This is untrue. The most dangerous creature of the night is the one that can still smile, speak gently and inspire trust. It does not always break down the door. Often it simply stands outside, waiting, and lets fear, grief or longing open it.

I have seen people drained not only of blood, but of will. First they grow pale. Then tired. Then silent, and at last obedient. They protect the thing that harms them, as though night itself has laid a hand upon their minds.

## Signs that a night creature is active in an area

Where such creatures move, certain signs may be found, if one dares to consider them together:

- People weaken without a known illness.
- Unshielded flames burn restlessly, especially late in the evening and before the cock crows.
- Animals, particularly dogs and horses, refuse to approach certain places.
- Children point towards dark corners and speak of “the man who only comes when the sun is gone”.
- Bare feet or hands leave few traces, as though the earth itself were reluctant to receive them.
- Unclothed corpses may appear peaceful, yet their skin is strangely empty, as though something has been drunk out of them.

Whoever searches for only one sign will doubt. Whoever finds many should prepare.

## Those who serve the night creatures

Not all who guard such creatures are themselves dead. Some still live, but live like dogs tethered to a dark table. I have seen them too.

They do not always bear visible wounds, but may be known by their gaze. Their eyes do not rest as they should. They sleep uneasily during the day and wake to unrest at sunset. They defend their master with greater fury than a son defends his mother.

Such servants are often found where people might otherwise ask questions: at cellars, storehouses, burial chambers, outbuildings or places where strangers should have no access.

If you find such men or women, know that they are dangerous — but also pitiable. Some may still be saved. Others are already so bound that they will die for the thing that owns them.

## The signs of a lair

These night creatures do not wander restlessly without shelter. They seek places that hide them from the day and allow them to reach the living at night. A lair is seldom recognised by one thing alone, but by an accumulation of unclean stillness:

- Cellars no one will open.
- Earth recently disturbed inside closed rooms.
- A smell of damp, old stone, blood and stale air.
- Carpets, hides or timber used to keep out light and draughts.
- Plain vessels, jars or bowls used to carry liquid in secret.
- A silence that seems affected, as though the house were holding its breath.
- People who enter, but seldom leave in daylight.

If such a place lies near a burial mound, an old stone chamber, an abandoned hall, a storehouse by the sea or an earth cellar, suspicion should grow.

## How they hunt

Most such creatures do not behave like wild beasts. They are patient. They choose the lonely, the grieving, the weak, the sick or those whom no one will believe.

They may gain shelter through words, lies, mercy or beauty. Once they have established a foothold, they often return to the same source of blood until it is broken in body or will.

They do not always claw. They do not always tear. Often they take a little at a time, to own more later.

Someone visited several nights in succession is seldom only a victim. They are becoming a doorway.

## Their weaknesses

Here I write what I have gathered from testimony, graves, old teachings and one night I would rather forget.

These beings do not all endure the same things equally, but much points in the same direction:

- Daylight is their enemy. Not as a priest is an enemy of sin, but as fire is an enemy of dry grass. Where sunlight reaches them, they weaken, and some perish.
- Fire frightens them and can destroy their shelter. A burning lair deprives them of the night's safety.
- Their resting place is the key. Unless you find where they rest, you are only chasing shadows. Find the place they seek as darkness fades, and you have taken hold of their throat.
- The head and heart must not be overlooked. I have read and heard that blows to the chest may halt them, but do not always finish the work. Many accounts suggest that binding or impaling the chest can hold the monster fast, but that a true end often requires more: fire, light and destruction of the body they use.
- Separation weakens them. Separate servant from master, victim from lair and creature from darkness. They are strong when no one dares stand together.

But I warn you: do not believe one old rule applies to all. Such beings are cunning, ancient and different. What binds one may only delay another.

## Counsel for those who would approach a lair

If anyone reads this with a hunt in mind, commit these words to memory:

- Never go alone. The solitary one disappears, and their name is whispered as a warning.
- Find where they rest by day. The king of night is daylight's rat.
- Watch for servants. Where you find obedient living people, darkness is already within.
- Close escape routes before you strike. A wounded night creature flees faster than fear.
- Use fire wisely. Burn the lair, not your friends.
- Stay together and speak truthfully. Such creatures live on division, fear and secrecy.
- If one of your own is bitten, drained or bewitched, do not let them go without a watchful eye. Darkness likes to come home in borrowed flesh.

The greatest mistake is to think this is like killing a wolf or a man. No. It is breaking something that has already broken the proper order.

## The scholar's final note

At first I thought this was devilry explained in poor words. Now I believe it is worse: a creature bearing enough of humanity to find its way into our hearts, and enough of death to despise everything living.

If these notes outlive me, let whoever finds them know this:

Fear not only the night. Fear the night that learns your name.

And if you find a place where the air stands still, where people wither without fever and where the door is tightly shut by day — break it open before the darkness within grows old.
