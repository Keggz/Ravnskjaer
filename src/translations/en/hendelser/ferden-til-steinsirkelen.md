## What waited at home

After the journey to Skjeggestad, the company wanted to go west again. This time, fewer would travel, and the aim was to explore the land and find the source of the pull Trym felt. The rest of Ravnskjær would prepare its defences and ready itself for winter.

Bjørn had also considered what they could do for the settlement upon returning. Jardar was offered command of the guards and reserves. Torstein would take responsibility for the hird, Asgeir for the hunting party, and Trym for spiritual life and rituals. Saia was offered a seat on the new small council, as a neutral voice from the hird who knew what was stirring among the people.

Åsmund, Ragna, Brynhild and Inga would also sit there. The offers accompanied the travellers westward as something to consider. They had not yet been accepted.

## Saia is given command

Bjørn placed the second westward voyage under Saia's command. Torstein was his son, and the jarl wished to avoid making the choice of leader look like a favour to his own house. By giving Saia responsibility, he could show that the undertaking belonged to Ravnskjær, not merely to the jarl's family.

The choice caused disagreement among those who were to travel. They did not agree on who should lead the company, and the jarl's decision did not immediately end the discussion. Nevertheless, Saia went with his commission and authority. She was to lead the voyage, even when the advice and wishes aboard differed.

## Words for the journey

Preparations took about a week. Trym, Bjarte, Asgeir, Jardar, Saia and Torstein made ready. Asgeir had studied under Hårek and felt more confident about finding the way across the sea, but his teacher did not join them this time. Asgeir steered the ship, with Jardar as his deputy and the others helping when needed. The box was brought along.

Between the journeys, they had spent time with the thralls and learned some English words, sounds and phrases. Now they hoped to make themselves understood without always having to resort to gestures.

Yrsa Åsmundsdatter and I had also progressed with the language. The parchments and writings from the first voyage could not be read in a single evening. We hoped to have more to present by winter, but the work was slow.

## The church near the stone building

The crossing went well. They aimed for roughly the same stretch of coast as before and then followed the land south, staying far enough out to keep the coast in sight.

After a few days, they found a church resembling the building on Lindisfarne. It appeared to stand without houses close around it. They landed at a small beach to its south and scouted. They then noticed armed people coming and going between the area and an immense stone building.

They waited until dark and watched the last force leave with torches. Then they crept over and helped one another up to look through the windows. Inside were seven unarmed monks.

The main door was the only entrance they found, and it was not barricaded. They stormed in and killed all seven. The plunder was smaller than at the first church: more writings and parchment scrolls, and some gold and silver.

Then torches appeared, heading towards them. They left the heaviest things behind and took what they could carry. They reached the boat without being stopped. The bells began ringing as they sailed on southward.

## Erte-Bert

Farther along the coast, they found a wrecked ship. Among the crates were silk and other costly goods. One man had survived; the rest of his company was dead.

He was a monk named Æthelberth, as the name was rendered to us. The travellers called him Erte-Bert. He had a warm disposition and was taken aboard along with what they salvaged from the wreck.

As he regained his strength, he managed to explain that his home lay on an island farther west. The company rounded the southern part of the land and continued along the coast.

## Mead on the fishing island

After a few days of calm sailing, they came to a small island with houses and fishing boats. Saia went first when they stepped ashore. She felt most confident with the language and spoke to a mature man and the others who met them. There were women, children and fishing folk, and none carried weapons.

With the words they had learned, they managed to understand one another reasonably well. This time, the encounter became a meal. The company offered mead, their hosts served a local stew, and celebrations followed.

They agreed to trade again if ships from Ravnskjær passed by. The name of this little place has not survived in the notes I write from. The agreement was later passed on to Åsmund.

## A promise on the island

Æthelberth's home was on the island recorded here as the Isle of Wight. The company put him ashore and ensured that he was cared for.

The monk had grown curious about them and about life in Ravnskjær. He asked them to stop and collect him on the way home. He wished to accompany them across the sea.

From this area, Trym felt the pull inland, towards the north-east. They brought the boat as far in as they could, found a suitable hiding place and continued on foot.

## The bathing warriors

The landscape opened into broad plains. One day, they saw two farm workers quarrelling until one knocked the other down. The company left them alone and moved on.

Later, they came upon soldiers bathing in a small lake. Their clothes and weapons lay on the shore. There was a fierce argument about whether to attack. Torstein wanted blood; Saia argued against it. Jardar and several others leaned towards her, and this time her advice was followed.

They waited until the men had finished and followed them. After a short walk, they saw that the road led towards a larger town. The company conferred and chose to approach the guards.

## Vatnaborg

There was a commotion of halting words and curious questions, but the welcome was friendly. Saia led the conversations. They paid for food and a place to sleep in the town they called Vatnaborg.

Asgeir traded his Ravnskjær bow for a longer, heavier one. Jardar, Torstein and Bjarte got drunk. Trym sought peace for his spiritual work, while Saia spoke to people and gathered information.

Here Bjarte met Eadgifu. They spent the night together, and he fell head over heels in love. When the company moved on, she stayed in the town, but Bjarte promised to return before the journey home.

They also learned where the stone formation could be found. People estimated four to six days' walking at a good pace. They were welcome to return to Vatnaborg.

## Between the stones

The way led through open plains, forest and marsh. Farms stood far apart. They hunted when they could, and around the evening fire they told of battles, honour and tall tales they had heard. Bjarte often spoke of Eadgifu.

At last, they reached the stone circle, Stonehenge, at midday. Before approaching, they made certain no one else was there. The size of the stones struck them. They were certain the place held significance, though they did not understand what it was.

They examined the stones and found signs by the formations of two upright stones with a crosspiece above. The signs resembled old runes, but not quite the ones they used themselves.

Jardar also worked with the box and managed to open it a little. They saw nothing much happen around them.

## Beneath the stone circle

As the sun began to set, they noticed a strange patch of earth outside the stone formation itself. Beneath it, they found a wooden hatch.

They lit torches and descended, with Bjarte in front. The passage was low, damp and cool, sloping beneath the earth. It felt long. At last, they reached a taller room containing a large, plain wooden chest.

The memory of Lindisfarne's burial chamber returned at once. They positioned themselves around the chest, ready to fight. Trym went closest and pushed the lid aside.

Long claws struck into his hand. He drew back wounded, and a thin, sinewy creature rose from the chest. It looked emaciated, with long claws and a face they could barely recognise as human. It attacked immediately.

The battle was hard. Several suffered serious injuries. Jardar was bitten in the neck and left with two marks. The creature was badly wounded too. Then it vanished before their eyes, as though their gaze could no longer hold it.

The sun had set. They ran after it and tried to follow the trail. The pursuit continued over several days; running shortened the return journey by a day and a half. But they lacked the strength to keep up with the creature's pace. It escaped them.

## Before the sun rose over Vatnaborg

When the company returned to Vatnaborg, it was still dark before dawn. The town was in chaos. Dead men, women and children lay in the gathering place, with guards and survivors assembled around them.

There was much talk they did not understand. But they could examine the wounds. The bodies were unusually pale, and several bore marks at the throat resembling Jardar's.

While they spoke with the living, a sound came from the dead behind them. A young person had risen from the heap and took a few steps towards them. Then the dawning light struck. The figure burned and sank into ash and wet remains.

The company suspected the creature they had encountered beneath the stones. I record the suspicion as it was told, not as something they had witnessed with their own eyes in the town that night.

## The wanderer in a foreign house

Bjarte found Eadgifu again. She decided to join them, and he welcomed her decision with joy.

Trym also sensed something familiar nearby. In a house some distance from the gathering place, he met Vilmar Vandreren. How had the man come all this way? The company had told no one which route they would take.

Vilmar hinted that there were other ways to travel, and others with whom one might travel. He urged them to keep protecting what they had found and said they would meet again soon.

He also spoke of threats that did not belong to this earth, and mentioned jötnar such as Eldrbrand, the destroyer of Steinvik. For Jardar, that name was tied to the town he came from and what he had lost there. The wanderer gave them new words to ponder, but little peace.

## Back across the sea

Fear marked the way back to the boat. At night, they kept watch in pairs, taking turns. When they reached the ship, they sailed to the Isle of Wight to collect Æthelberth as agreed.

Saia went ashore for him. The other scholars and monks seemed confused by his departure, but he came along. A strong friendship grew between him and Saia.

The journey home was long. They stayed off the coast before turning out onto the open sea. The weather grew harsh and the waves rough, but all came through alive. No further supernatural encounters are recorded from this crossing.

In Ravnskjær, they were warmly received. Æthelberth was given lodging, Eadgifu moved in with Bjarte, and Åsmund heard about the trade agreement and the goods they could obtain in exchange. They had come home with more than cargo: with new connections, new wounds and questions that followed them all the way into winter.
