## The days among the ruins

After Eldrbrand, there was work for every hand that could still be raised. The company cleared the wreckage alongside Vilmar, Eadgifu and those who had survived at Ravnskjær. The dead had to be gathered and laid with dignity. The living needed somewhere to stay.

The boulders could not be moved with the strength we had left. Vilmar said he would do something about them. Late one night, while the others slept, he dealt with them through his art. How, I have no eyewitness account to tell.

These were not days filled with the joy of victory. Grief lay heavily over the settlement, together with a question no amount of work could put to rest: how could we protect our people when something like that could come over the mountains?

## What emerged over meals

While they were still waiting for the rest of the people to come home, the company gave Vilmar little peace at mealtimes. They asked about what they had seen and everything they now feared might exist. He had turned the sea against Eldrbrand. They wanted to know how many other truths he carried with him.

It emerged that Eadgifu's journey to Ravnskjær had not been merely a matter of chance. She too belonged to Askebærerne. She had no supernatural abilities like Vilmar's, but possessed an open mind and knowledge of alchemy and healing. Bjarte did not take the discovery badly.

Ben-Gerd had also worked closely with Askebærerne for a long time. Yet she had remained something of her own, and no one reduced her to merely a part of their order. Eadgifu said her hut did not feel empty. What she sensed there was never clearly explained.

Vilmar spoke of the Penumbra, of Gaia and of the powers he called the Wyld, the Weaver and the Wyrm. What the company had seen beyond the door of fire was only a glimpse of a far larger world. I record the names as they were given; everything said about them cannot fit within this account.

The dangers acquired names as well. There were werewolves and others who could take animal forms, bears among them. There were vampires, mages, evil spirits and beings Vilmar called fomori. The company had sought knowledge to feel safer. Now, at least, they knew more about how much they still could not protect themselves against.

## The box and the road north

They raised the question of the box. Vilmar said it had to be taken to Jotunheimen, far north and high in the mountains. They still had no certain destination they could point to.

He took the key fragments with him. The company hesitated to hand them over, but Vilmar said they had to be taken as far from the box as possible. He himself would travel southeast, as far as he could go.

He helped them through those days before leaving. When he finally departed with the fragments, it was a farewell whose full meaning none of them understood. No one saw him again after this.

The box remained with the company. Its journey would have to wait.

## The boats for the dead

Only after Vilmar had departed did the remaining evacuees return. They found scorched earth, the dead and shattered houses. Some wept. Others searched for their own or tried to understand what their eyes were showing them. The accounts of the creature were hard to grasp; fear and confusion did nothing to make them clearer. But people understood that the hird and the guards had kept Ravnskjær from being utterly destroyed.

The small council quickly decided that Torstein should take over as interim jarl, until an allthing could gather and make the formal decision. Ravnskjær needed someone to give orders while grief was still fresh.

Torstein asked those who could to prepare boats for the funeral. After a few days, the work was done. On one of their largest boats they built an especially large platform for Bjørn Ulvsson, the jarl who had fallen defending the settlement.

Inga laid beside him a piece of jewellery he had given her. Torstein returned a sword to his father, a gift Bjørn had once given his son. Around them, people brought objects that had belonged to or mattered to each of the dead. Names accompanied the belongings. A life could not be gathered into a boat, but they gave what they could.

When the boats were sent out to sea, the flaming arrows were ready. All eyes turned to Torstein. He hesitated. Words had never been his easiest weapon to wield, but he found enough to say what the people needed to hear. I shall not give him a speech he never made; those who stood there remembered that his words did their work.

Asgeir shot. The arrows found their mark and set the boats alight as intended, on the first attempt. The people stood watching them burn out on the water.

Afterwards they gathered outside for the funeral feast, with food and mead. There was room for celebration and memories among the tears. Grief did not end because a cup was filled. But it was borne together.

## New hands in the hird

The work continued. Ravnskjær had to be raised again, and the few defenders left needed more people beside them.

The summer games were held as well. What the participants showed there formed the basis for choosing new warriors for the hird. These five were selected:

- **Ymril Faunsdatter**, an axe warrior from [Askvin](/en/places/askvin), east of Ravnskjær.
- **Ulrik den Lille**, from [Hrafnvik](/en/places/hrafnvik) in the north.
- **Alrik den Store**, Ulrik's twin, also from [Hrafnvik](/en/places/hrafnvik).
- **Harald Hårmagre**, an archer from [Kvalnes](/en/places/kvalnes), north of Ravnskjær.
- **Geir Fieri**, a warrior with a hand axe from [Haugnes](/en/places/haugnes) in the south.

These were names to count on when watches had to be posted and weapons carried again. The dead could not be replaced. Even so, the gaps in the defence had to be filled if the living were to remain.

Before winter set in, the people had managed to rebuild Ravnskjær. That did not mean all work was finished, or that the wounds within its people had healed.

## The Silver Sceptic returns

Early that autumn, Øystæinn «Sølvskeptikeren» Spjótaldr returned after several years away from the settlement. He was Åsmund's brother, and regarded the new leadership with clear scepticism.

Vali, Sjur, Olve and Einar took a strong liking to him and listened to his views on what had happened and how Ravnskjær should be governed. Autumn thus also brought questions that could not be answered with a palisade or a sword.

Øystæinn was alive when he returned. His later fate belongs to another part of the story.

## The winter before the long journey

The winter that followed was cold. The final harvests had yielded less than expected, even though the seed had been good. The cause had not been established.

Where Eldrbrand had fallen, the earth still held its heat. Snow did not settle as it did elsewhere, but slowly melted against the black ground. The jotunn's body was gone; his mark remained among us.

Torstein ordered alliances to be secured wherever possible. Ties with [Askvin](/en/places/askvin), [Hrafnvik](/en/places/hrafnvik), [Kvalnes](/en/places/kvalnes) and [Haugnes](/en/places/haugnes) were to be strengthened, and more warriors brought into the defence. Skjeggestad would be allowed to govern and manage its own affairs.

The journey to Jotunheimen was postponed until winter was over. They expected to be away for several months, perhaps longer, and still did not know precisely where they were going. They would not begin such a journey north and into the mountains in the winter cold.

So the winter of 794–795 passed in further work, alliances, training and preparation against dangers they could now name, and others they still did not know. Ravnskjær stood again. This time we knew more about what it might cost to keep it standing.
