I call the island Lindisfarne in this account. The travellers did not know its name when they set foot there. Only later, when the monk Ceadda taught me some English and Latin, did I learn the name and become able to record it.

## The jarl's summons and Ben-Gerd's vision

After the games, the jarl called the inhabitants to food and celebration. His message was heavier than the meal: Ravnskjær did not have enough provisions to survive the winter. Something had to be done while there was still time.

Bjørn said he had visited Ben-Gerd and received visions of land and wealth across the sea to the west. Now he asked for volunteers for an expedition. Torstein, Jardar, Asgeir, Trym and Saia stepped forward, joined by men and women from the hird.

Ulf Blodøye, Kjartan Klippen, Sigurd Galeis, Ragnhild Skjoldmøy and Finn Tjugeskalle were among the company. Hårek Stjernesyn would find the way across the sea. Sjur Vidarsson, Olve Vidarsson, Einar Hemmingsson and Vali Svensson also joined — four young boys in a company soon to be tested.

Before departing, the travellers visited Ben-Gerd. They later told of visions of unfamiliar signs, a cross and a building they did not yet know. The signs had been seen; their meaning had not been understood.

Supplies were prepared, and the travellers consulted Knut En-øye. Thus the voyage took shape: not merely as a search for riches, but as an attempt to give Ravnskjær a winter it could survive.

## The sea took Kjartan

On the first day, they met a ship coming from the south and heading north. Its captain spoke for a while with Finn Tjugeskalle. The others did not know his name then; in the records, he appears as Ola «Sjørev» Grimsson.

There was talk that the Tjugeskalle people were not satisfied with Jarl Bjørn. The travellers noticed the conversation and the rumours. What else was said or agreed between the men is not recorded here.

Later came the storm. The ship struck a great wave, and Kjartan Klippen lost his grip or his balance. The great warrior was swept overboard. They never saw him again.

Olve Vidarsson nearly followed him. Asgeir Eriksson put his own life at risk and caught the boy. Olve was saved.

Thus the company was diminished before foreign land was reached: one man lost to the sea, one boy held back from the same fate.

## The raven on the mast

After the storm, thick fog lay over the sea. Out there, they found a ship drifting without clear direction. A raven perched at the top of its mast, watching them.

Jardar, Torstein and Asgeir went aboard. They found few objects, but among them were a jar containing ash and bones, an amulet and signs they did not understand.

Torstein took the amulet with him. Asgeir tried to set fire to the ship before they moved on.

I have no explanation to add. The raven sat there, the ship drifted, and the amulet went with them. What these things had to do with one another, they did not know then.

## The farms without people

Birds in the air heralded land before the coast itself came into view. They sailed south along it and found a small beach with fishing equipment, crates and smaller boats. There they made camp and sent people to explore the land.

A simple path led between small farmhouses, fields and grazing cows and sheep. There were no people to be seen.

The boys stayed by the ship with Ragnhild Skjoldmøy and Sigurd Galeis to guard the camp. The others moved cautiously onwards and examined the houses one by one. The cooking pots were still partly warm. The houses were lived in, but their inhabitants were gone.

Then Saia spotted two figures in the distance. She waved. They stopped for a moment, dropped what they were carrying and ran back the way they had come.

The company gave chase. Beyond a long, gentle slope, they gained a view of a cluster of about thirteen houses and buildings. On the higher ground beyond stood the house they recognised from Ben-Gerd's visions.

Saia and Trym tried to make contact with the people below. The answer was flight. Many ran west. Some went up to the large building to raise the alarm, and soon the bells rang out over Lindisfarne.

## When the bells rang

They passed through the settlement and climbed to the house on the hill. We now call it the church; then, to them, it was chiefly the building from the visions. The main door was barricaded. At the back was another entrance.

Jardar, Finn and Trym took the rear way in. They passed through the storeroom into the kitchen, while those inside — mostly monks and scholars — focused on the main door. There, the rest of the company worked their way through the heavy wooden doors.

When the door gave way, they stood facing one another. Saia tried to speak. Then an axe flew through the air and struck the monk nearest her. Who threw it is not recorded in this account.

After that, most were killed. Those who fled the first room through the kitchen met the people who had entered from behind. A few monks escaped. One was captured to be made a thrall.

### Warriors approaching

The pause was brief. Haakon Haukøye spotted figures marching towards them. Eight men were counted as warriors. Asgeir sent three arrows towards them, and one arrow brought down a man.

The fighting that followed was hard, but the company prevailed without losing any more of their own. They suffered wounds and scrapes. Trym's attempt at an acrobatic manoeuvre ended with him falling on his face.

I record both the victory and what came before it. That none of our people fell in this fight does not make the church floor any less bloody.

## The box beneath the church

Once the most urgent wounds had been tended, they began gathering whatever could be used: food, seeds, valuables, and intact weapons and armour. But beneath the storeroom, something other than provisions waited.

Jardar, Torstein, Asgeir and Saia discovered a hidden hatch and climbed down a narrow ladder. Meanwhile, Trym helped gather plunder from the houses and farms.

Below lay a burial chamber. They lit torches fixed to the walls and saw dead people laid in the walls and in fragile wooden coffins. There were parchment scrolls and texts in a language they could not read. Shelves concealed a narrow passage, so tight they had to go through one at a time.

### The great dead man

The passage led into a larger chamber containing an ornate stone coffin. Four torches were lit before the coffin was opened. The dead man filled almost its entire length; they estimated him at nearly two and a half metres.

On his chest lay a box, held between his hands. Jardar took it.

Then the dead man opened his eyes and fixed them on him. Three other dead awoke in the chamber behind them. What had been a search for plunder became a fight to climb out alive.

They won, but barely. All four suffered serious injuries. The great draugr's long claws tore Jardar's chest and left scars. Afterwards, their strength was spent.

In the inventory of finds, the box is described as a cube with four openings resembling keyholes. A key was also among the finds from the great dead man. What the box contained, and what the key could open, I do not answer here.

## A heavy cargo homeward

Before they could sail, everything had to be collected and carried to the ship. The morning and afternoon were spent on the work: the finds from the church and cellar, from the farms and houses, and from the dead.

There was more to find than gold. In the stores were meat, fish, honey, cheese and drink. There were grain and seeds — wheat, barley, oats, rye and flax — and other things that could matter to a settlement with failing harvests. Silver, gold, church objects, books and parchment were also listed among the finds.

But an inventory of what exists is not the same as an inventory of what a ship can carry. Cargo had to be weighed against space, seaworthiness and Bjørn Ulvsson's commission. Not everything that glitters feeds a family, and an overloaded ship brings nothing home.

### Those who did not travel willingly

Three people were taken as thralls: a monk and two who had hidden in their homes. In the list, they are recorded as Ceadda Gummer, Wigmund Osmer and Hilda Balston.

They too were part of what was brought to the ship. Let it be written that they had not chosen the journey themselves.

At last the cargo was aboard, and the sail was raised. Out at sea, they saw several people standing on shore, watching them as the distance grew.

## What had been taken

On the homeward voyage, there was hope aboard. The cargo gave them reason to believe Ravnskjær could face the winter with something other than empty stores. Then the thick fog returned.

There lay the ship again, with the raven on the mast. It had not burned down. Asgeir's attempt to destroy it had not prevented this new encounter.

The three who had boarded it heard voices from the ship, though no one could be seen. The message kept returning: what had been taken must be put back. They told the others. No explanation accompanied the demand.

## From the depths of the sea

The rest of the voyage was calmer until roughly one day remained. Then several arms reached up from the sea and gripped the ship. The creature beneath them was enormous and deep red.

They called it a sea monster and sought words for it among the powers that ruled the sea and nature. I name no god behind the attack. What they told me was that they fought, wounded the creature and made it let go. It vanished into the depths.

## Home to Ravnskjær

In the late summer or autumn of that same year, the company returned to a settlement gathered to meet them. Bjørn Ulvsson welcomed them with Inga and the family. He spoke of their courage and what they had done for Ravnskjær. Kjartan Klippen, whom the sea had taken, was also mentioned.

The cargo was carried ashore. Later, the settlement gathered for a great meal, and the celebration went well. But Saia had noticed that not every face bore the same joy. Among the Tjugeskalle people, she saw discontent that the voyage had succeeded.

There was other news too. Tor had vanished while the ship was away. Wolves were seen more often around the settlement, and some of the animals were said to be unusually large. There were rumours of meetings between the Tjugeskalle people and scouts from Skjeggestad, where Ola Sjørev was based.

Thus ended the first westward voyage: with food and wealth brought ashore, with Kjartan's place empty, and with unrest already waiting at home's threshold.
