It was summer in the year 793, but winter was already in our thoughts. The harvests had failed for several years. Even so, Jarl Bjørn Ulvsson declared the summer games open, and for a while the settlement had something to count besides what was missing from its stores.

Axes were thrown for distance. Great boulders had to be lifted and carried along a course, and the players met in fierce competition at knattleikr. People wagered possessions on those they thought would win. There was life and good humour around the games, and several who would later sail west distinguished themselves in the contests.

I record their names here: Torstein, Jardar, Asgeir, Trym and Saia.

But the day also took its due. Egil den svake, a thrall, was crushed beneath his own boulder. Tor took his place in the games. It is easy to remember who won. Egil's name must not fall out of the account either.
