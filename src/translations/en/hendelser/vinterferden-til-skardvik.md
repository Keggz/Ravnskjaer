## The message from the north

Deep into the winter of 793–794, when the cold was at its harshest, Bjørn summoned the warriors to the longhouse. A message had come from Skardvik. Our northern allies needed help at once. What had struck them was still unclear, but the entire village was in danger.

The jarl knew Åskell Helgesson as a man who did not ask lightly for such help. He asked whether the company would travel through the snow and cold. None of the six hesitated.

Asgeir, Trym, Bjarte, Saia, Jardar and Torstein gathered provisions, skis, snowshoes and poles. They packed what they thought would last a week's journey there and back. Bjarte said farewell to Eadgifu and promised to come home. Torstein took leave of his family, Jardar of Gunhild. Asgeir would guide them. He had been to Skardvik before, but never in winter conditions like these.

What follows, I record from what they told me upon their return.

## Through the winter country

They travelled while there was light. The road north was hard and steep, and daylight gave them few hours to use. Small farms and cabins became shelter for the night when they could find them. When they could not reach roof and walls, they dug into the snow and lit fires.

After several days of toil, they reached the outskirts of Skardvik. Between the houses, all was quiet. No one came to meet them. Only smoke from the longhouse showed that living people still remained inside.

The windows were barricaded. The company knocked and called out that Ravnskjær had answered the summons. Behind the door, they heard movement and a frightened voice asking whether they were truly alive, and whether they belonged with the ice-cold figure outside.

At last, a weary older man opened the door a crack. When he saw who had come, he quickly brought them inside.

## Those who still lived

Skardvik had been a village of about fifty people, with small houses gathered around the longhouse. Now those who had escaped sat behind the barricades. Among them were Jarl Åskell and his daughter, Solveig.

The hunter Borunn told of a figure that had been seen around the village. It stood watching people without caring about the cold. Then one night had brought chaos. People died, rose again and attacked whoever stood nearest. About half the village was said to have fallen during the first attack. The others had managed to shut themselves inside.

Since then, attacks had come every day. The guards were exhausted, and the food would barely last another day. Åskell and Solveig asked whether more people were coming from Ravnskjær. The help before them was six warriors.

The company conferred with them. Erling Trefot, Kara, the brothers Falki and Arnvid, and Borunn stepped forward. They would help secure the windows and protect those who could not resist on their own.

## Tor in the blizzard

Trym sensed that something was near. The six went outside and asked the others to close the door behind them. Trym, Saia and Asgeir stayed near the entrance. Jardar, Torstein and Bjarte ventured farther between the houses.

In the storm stood a gaunt man, naked and covered in ice. When the snow eased for a moment, they recognised him: Trellen Tor, who had vanished from Ravnskjær while the company was on its first westward voyage.

No ordinary man could have stood like that in the cold. When they spoke to him, something answered through him that the one they sought was no longer there. It was cold inside now.

Trym collapsed. Around them, cold bodies rose from the snow and began drawing nearer. Those who told me of it estimated their number at about fifty. Torstein wanted to advance towards Tor, but the others shouted that they must retreat and get Trym under shelter.

They made it inside before the pounding began against the doors. Soon the dead were striking the windows too. The children screamed, and fear spread through the hall.

## Over the roof

Those with battle experience were each given an opening to defend. Anything that came through was to be driven back. The company believed this must be a spiritual power, and Solveig went to Trym to help him. Åskell spoke to the people and kept hope alive while the walls bore the blows.

With Bjarte's help, Asgeir climbed through the roof. From there, he saw Tor behind the longhouse, farther among the small houses. Those able to follow him came after and jumped down at the back.

The dead were occupied with breaking in. The company crept close to Tor, threw him to the ground and bound him. Then the dead turned towards them. The bodies moved slowly enough for them to return through one of the rear windows before their path was cut off.

They had brought Tor inside. Now they had to keep the attackers out long enough to do something about what dwelt within him.

## The time Solveig asked for

Trym lay by the fire, safely clear of the flames, and shook without stopping. The warmth could not reach the cold that had settled in him. Solveig asked them to lay Tor beside him.

She had heard of spirits taking people, but had never seen it herself. She knew a ritual that might help. It would require time and a sacrifice. What the sacrifice would be, she did not say then.

The rest took their places at the doors and windows. Everyone who could hold a weapon was asked to help. Bera and Hemming were among those who resisted. Their son, Arle, was also in the hall.

Bjarte and Saia stood at the door. Around them, the wood began to crack. Solveig started her work, and every moment she gained had to be fought for by the others.

## When the barricades fell

The door gave way first. Then the windows fell, one by one. The dead poured in, more than the defenders could hold back. Solveig shouted that she needed more time.

Bera and Hemming were killed. Arle was about to be taken when Jardar saw him. He reached the boy and barely managed to save him.

Erling Trefot fell. Kara, only twelve years old, was killed. Falki and Arnvid were dragged down, and Borunn lost her life. The company had to retreat ever farther into the hall. Åskell was mortally wounded, but still alive.

These names shall stand here. They were people the company had spoken with that day, and they held the attackers back for as long as they could.

## Life against the cold

Solveig saw her father's wounds and asked him to come closer. They held one another amid the battle. No one has told me what they said.

She drove the knife into his side and held him as he sank towards the floor. Then she opened his chest and took out his heart. It was Åskell's life she gave as the sacrifice the ritual required.

With her hand against Tor's frozen chest, she called him back. The words have been recounted thus:

> Tor, son of humankind, hear me.\
> The cold has taken you, but life still knows you.
>
> I call the warmth that once dwelt in your heart.\
> I call the memory of fire, of breath, of summer's blood.
>
> Spirit of frost, release him.\
> Náðskrímr, loosen your grip.\
> This shall not be your vessel.

She pressed the heart against Tor's chest and finished:

> Life goes to life.\
> Blood breaks ice.\
> Come back to us, Tor.

The dead collapsed. The blows against the defenders ceased. Tor awoke for a brief moment, as did Trym, but neither had the strength to remain awake.

Then the hall fell silent.

## What they brought home

About twelve more had lost their lives in the final battle, Åskell among them. Why Skardvik had been struck, and how Tor had fallen into the spirit's grip, no one could answer with certainty.

The survivors cleared the longhouse and carried the dead outside. Food was too scarce for them to stay. They gathered skis, snowshoes, provisions and other useful equipment, rested one more night and prepared to leave the village.

Jardar took Arle under his wing. The boy had lost both parents. He said little, but understood that Jardar would care for him.

The return journey was easier than the journey north. All the survivors reached Ravnskjær in reasonably good condition. Tor and Trym remained severely weakened, and the cold did not release them during the first nights at home either.

The dead were left outside the houses in Skardvik. The company said they would return when the weather grew milder and give them a proper burial.

I must add what was later neglected: none of them returned to do it, and no one else was sent. The promise remains unfulfilled.
