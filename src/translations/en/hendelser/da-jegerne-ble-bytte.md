## The road to Myrkagap

A few days after the feast, Asgeir, Saia, Trym, Jardar, Bjarte and Torstein set out with Øystæinn as their guide. They had packed provisions for roughly a week's journey there and back, provided nothing delayed them. They knew where they meant to go, but not how much the cave would demand of them.

There were no great incidents on the road. Yet they noticed that wildlife grew scarcer as they approached Blodskjæft. It was an absence worth noticing, though none could explain it with certainty.

I did not travel with them. What follows was gathered from those who returned. They remembered much of the fear and pain, but not every blow in the order it fell.

## Those who brought goods

They reached the area some time before sunset. From what they had learned in the parchment, they wanted as much daylight as possible when they entered. So they waited.

While the light lasted, they examined the terrain. Then they withdrew well away from the cave and took turns watching from a distance. If the creatures emerged in the dark, they did not want to be found beside the entrance.

Through their watches, they saw people coming and going with goods, from north, east and south. Some carried supplies in; others took goods away. What they saw suggested connections far beyond Øystæinn's trading. The creatures they had come to find did not show themselves.

Øystæinn told them more about the goods and services he had provided. But he had never gone beyond the first chamber. There the knowledge of their guide also ended.

They slept poorly. When morning came, they prepared to enter early, while the day still lay ahead of them.

## Where daylight ended

The entrance was a narrow opening between black rock faces. Water stained the stone with rust. The smell was raw and cold, of old earth, damp and iron.

They moved slowly, making as little noise as possible. Trade goods filled the first chambers, and water dripped from the ceiling. Daylight reached far less deeply than they had hoped. They had to light torches.

Old weapons and shields lay scattered about, with heaps of bones along the sides. Dark, dried stains proved to be blood. Passages led onward in several directions, and the cave reached farther in than any of them had imagined.

They went right, towards the sound of running water. There the cave opened onto a dark pool. Bones lay gathered at its edge, and red trails reached out into the water. The route continued around the pool and farther inward before dividing between a chamber on the right and a passage to the left. The sound of falling stones came from the depths.

A large, closed stone coffin stood in the chamber, its lid carved with patterns. The company remembered what a coffin beneath Stonehenge had brought them. They left this one alone.

Beyond it, they found three sleeping places covered with hides, and urns and barrels gathered along the rock wall. Several urns were empty, but had previously held something. Clanging and other sounds then drew them towards a chamber near the centre. A round stone altar marked with symbols along its edge stood at the meeting of several passages. A corpse had been cut open beside it, and blood ran out along channels through the cave.

None of them could still believe this was merely a hidden trading place.

## Between two darknesses

The sounds came from a chamber farther within. The passage was narrow; at its widest two could fit, and at its narrowest they had to go one at a time.

Jardar, Bjarte and Torstein squeezed through first into the room beyond. There was more space here, with benches, weapon racks and round shields along the sides. Trym stood closest to the opening behind them, followed by Saia and Asgeir. Øystæinn was at the rear, nearer the altar chamber.

Before the first three stood a tall, pale, slender figure. Its skin reminded them of old bone beneath wet earth. The face might almost have been beautiful, were it not for the hollow cheeks, the mouth and the dark eyes. It watched them without haste. When it moved, it seemed simply to be somewhere else, silent and far too quick.

Trym became aware of something above him. He had time to look up before another figure detached itself from the darkness of the ceiling and dropped onto him. It was lean and sinewy, grey-skinned, with coarse nails darkened by old blood and a jaw that seemed too powerful for its face.

Behind Saia and Asgeir, a third pale face appeared between the rocks. Thin hair clung to its skin. Its head tilted as though listening to their pulses. Then it was gone, before appearing elsewhere, closer than it should have been able to reach.

They had come to hunt. Now they stood scattered through a narrow passage, with enemies ahead, above and behind.

## What they held on to

The battle that followed was not remembered as a succession of clean victories. There were cramped rooms, torchlight and bodies coming too close. None of the six escaped it unhurt.

Jardar had his spear, Bjarte his two-handed axe and Torstein his two swords in the innermost chamber. Behind them, Trym fought with an axe, Saia with a sword and Asgeir with his bow. Weapons had to be used wherever there was room, while darkness made every opening uncertain. Øystæinn tried to get away.

Afterwards they said their will kept them standing when their strength should no longer have been enough. All six suffered wounds so severe that death was close. Still they fought on.

Saia lost an eye. A terrible wound crossed her face. The others were marked as well; scars and memories of that cave would remain in their bodies for a long time.

At last the three vampires were beheaded, following the knowledge the company had brought from the parchment. Who struck each of the final blows is not remembered with certainty. I will not grant one man or woman credit for what none can now clearly recount.

Øystæinn had been killed. The other six still lived, but had to staunch their own bleeding before they could do much else. Trym was among the survivors; his later death belongs to another sequence of events.

## The coins they did not know

Once they had brought the bleeding under control, they examined what lay in the cave. There were trade goods, silver, metals, weapons, armour and shields. They could not carry everything and took only what they could manage.

Some small coins drew particular interest. Their design was unfamiliar, but the markings reminded them of something English. They intended to show them to Æthelberth when they returned. What the coins meant, they did not yet know.

They had left the stone coffin alone. Defeating what they had fought did not answer every question raised by what they had seen.

## The one they carried home

They secured Øystæinn's body and brought him back to Ravnskjær with dignity. The man who had set out as their guide returned carried by those he had meant to help.

All six returned, badly wounded. They had slain the creatures in Blodskjæft, but victory had cost a life and left its marks on each of them.

The coins awaited interpretation. Øystæinn had to be brought home to his own. And those who had read of night creatures at a feast table now knew something no parchment alone could teach them.
