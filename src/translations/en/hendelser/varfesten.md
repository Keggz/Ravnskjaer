## When winter loosened its grip

After the long winter, the sun began to warm Ravnskjær again. Grief for what Eldrbrand had taken remained, but the days offered more light to work by.

The company had tried to reach the ulfhednar with smoke signals. No one answered. They needed counsel about what lay beyond the visible world, but asking did not bring a reply.

Bjarte and Eadgifu grew ever closer. In Torstein and Tilde's home, the child born the previous summer was growing; the name has not yet been entered in these records. Jardar cared for Gunhild and Arle and worked on new watch schedules, posts and defences. Saia attended the small council and tended her horse. Trym spent much time with the thralls from England and with me, learning languages and trying to understand the dangers of the other side. He continued studying Latin with Æthelberth.

Asgeir and Brynhild had provided meat through the winter. Brynhild now governed Skjeggestad while they kept the hunting going.

There was talk of more wolves near the settlement. Some said they had heard laughter from Ben-Gerd's hut. Others spoke of trading Øystæinn was doing beyond the business people knew about. He himself grew thinner and looked increasingly ill. The rumours offered no certain explanation for any of it.

## The feast after the allthing

The allthing decided that Torstein should retain his power as jarl. When early summer came in 795, he wanted to gather Ravnskjær for a feast. The people needed to remember what they had endured, and the clans needed an evening that could strengthen their ties.

The tables stood outdoors. Torstein first sat beside Inga, but soon joined the rest of the company and the hird. Spirits were high, and the mead went round.

Many could see what happened at the tables. What later took place in the forest and behind the door of Bjarte's house, I record from the company's account.

## Asgeir follows

Asgeir noticed Øystæinn behaving strangely and slipping away from the feast. He followed him into the forest, far enough behind to remain concealed.

In the darkness, Øystæinn met some men beside goods that seemed to have been placed there beforehand. From a distance, Asgeir could not make out everything. They looked around, completed their meeting and parted without discovering him.

When Øystæinn returned along the same path, Asgeir hid and let him pass. He waited until he believed the place was empty, then went forward.

There were crates of assorted trade goods, rope and small urns. Some urns were empty. Others held liquid. Asgeir examined one and recognised blood. Whom or what it had come from, he could not tell.

Then he headed back towards Ravnskjær with what he had learned.

## The parchment and the trouble at the table

While Asgeir was away, I came to Trym with a text I believed to be of great importance. He had asked me to watch for such things, and here were words about creatures of the night, their servants and their hiding places. The writing was attributed to Brother Matthaeus of Lindisfarne.

Trym became absorbed in it. The others could not see what held him so closely to the text and teased him for sitting like a brooding owl in the middle of the feast. Later, they would listen far more attentively.

At the Tjugeskalle table, Bjarte saw that Rask Finnson was troubling Eadgifu. He went over at once and sat beside Rask. When Rask made crude remarks about what he wanted to do to Eadgifu, Bjarte lost his temper. He slammed Rask's head into the table and threw him over it.

The feast stopped. Bjarte stood ready for more, while Rask took longer to get up. But Bjarte brought Eadgifu back to the hird. Rask sat with his own people, and the talking and drinking resumed.

## What the clans knew

Jardar and Saia moved between the tables to talk to people and keep spirits up. Saia sat with Åsmund and asked about Øystæinn.

Åsmund believed his brother was involved in something. He had bought so many urns, ropes and other goods that Åsmund had to bring in more from elsewhere. He could not understand what it was all for. But trading ran in the family, he thought. He had also noticed the change in Øystæinn's appearance, without knowing whether it came from anything beyond stress and illness.

Jardar sat for a while with Ragna and Vera among the Torløv clan. Torstein also made the rounds among the clans; now he had to tend those relationships as jarl, not merely as someone who knew the people at the tables.

Øystæinn returned and sat with Åsmund and their people. Once Asgeir was back as well, the company gathered and asked the rest of the hird to mingle with the guests. They needed a few words in private.

Asgeir told them about the meeting and the blood. Saia shared what Åsmund had said. Trym went through the parchment: the signs of night creatures, people who could be bound to them, and the warning against believing all such beings were alike.

## A plan drowned in mead

They intended to wait until later in the evening and get Øystæinn alone. Torstein, Bjarte and Jardar would speak to him. But Øystæinn was not the only one who had drunk well, and the plan grew less careful when it was put into action.

Before the assembled guests, Torstein announced that Øystæinn stood accused of unlawful dealings. Then they took him away. Åsmund protested. Jardar tried to calm him by saying they only wanted to speak to his brother and that he was not accused of anything.

One statement did not undo the other. Torstein had long wanted to be jarl. Now his words carried a weight he had not yet fully learned to recognise.

Saia, Asgeir and Trym stayed to keep the feast going, speaking with the guests, Rolf Bakstein among them. The other three went on with Øystæinn, who was deeply affected by the mead. He thought they were going to continue drinking together and offered them as much as they wanted. He said he only wanted to be their friend.

Torstein lost patience and struck him from behind, knocking him unconscious. They carried him into Bjarte's house and bound him.

## What Øystæinn told them

When Øystæinn awoke, he refused to speak. Jardar fetched Saia, Asgeir and Trym. They objected to what had been done: the jarl and his men had struck down and bound the brother of one of Ravnskjær's most powerful clan leaders. This could sow division far beyond that evening.

Saia saw that the matter had become about more than Øystæinn. It was about trust. Ravnskjær had lost Bjørn, the harvests were failing and dark rumours were spreading. At such a time, people would ask not only what was true, but who appeared reassuring, fair and strong.

She also understood how precarious the balance around the Helga clan had become. Press them too hard, and bitterness could make them dangerous. Treat them too gently, and others might believe the jarl protected his own or did not dare to act. How Øystæinn was treated could therefore have consequences for all of Ravnskjær.

It was this danger that made Saia react so strongly. The discussion grew heated, and Torstein argued with her, Asgeir and Trym. While they quarrelled, Bjarte sat beside Øystæinn and offered him something to drink. Then he began to talk.

He had wanted friends. Before he understood what he had become involved in, he had been forced to work for creatures dwelling in a cave called [Blodskjæft](/en/places/blodskjaeft) by Myrkagap, a few days' journey to the northeast. According to him, they had contacts beyond him alone.

They threatened those close to him unless he cooperated. They wanted to know who governed Ravnskjær, how the clans stood towards one another and what else was happening in the settlement. They also ordered many kinds of trade goods and were willing to pay. Øystæinn did not know why they were gathering all this knowledge.

On his neck were marks the company recognised from England. From these and the parchment, Trym believed the creatures had made him a “spawn”, a bound servant. This was Trym's deduction; the full nature of what had been done to Øystæinn was not yet established.

## The brothers and the apology

Bjarte interrupted the argument and took the others aside to tell them what he had heard. The company agreed to fetch Åsmund. Asgeir went to bring him to the house.

Torstein met them on the way. He explained what had happened and offered Åsmund a full apology for his conduct. He said he had meant to protect Ravnskjær.

Øystæinn would help them and guide them to Blodskjæft. Before they returned, the brothers had a moment together. I need invent none of their words here.

Then everyone went back to the feast. Bjarte sat with Rask and drank with him. The others mingled again. The evening had held violence, suspicion and an apology; still they sat beneath the same sky and tried to remain a community.

## The road towards Blodskjæft

The following day moved slowly. Mead lingered in many bodies, and when the company gathered late in the day, it was not only their heads that hurt. They also had the previous night's actions to consider.

They agreed to take Øystæinn with them as soon as they were fit to travel. The threat near Myrkagap had to be faced. A few days later they had gathered what they needed and made themselves ready.

The parchment had offered advice they hoped would give them an advantage. Whether that advice would hold against what awaited them in the cave, they did not yet know.

Here ends the account of the feast. The journey to Blodskjæft will have its own telling.
