## A farewell by the sea

Once again the company came home with a man whose death they did not know how to explain. So it had been with Finn Tjugeskalle. Now it was Øystæinn who must be returned to his kin.

From Blodskjæft they had also brought the vampires' bodies and severed heads. These were shown only to the small council. They bore witness to what the company had faced, but offered no simple explanation for everything that had happened.

Øystæinn received a funeral worthy of his standing. Before his journey carried him out to sea, Åsmund spoke of his brother. Of his words, I would preserve these:

> My brother Øystæinn was no easy man. He did not trust readily, speak softly or bow to grand words merely because they were spoken loudly in the hall.

Åsmund remembered a man about whom many had held strong opinions, and whom more would judge after death. He asked the living to bear their grief and stand together through the hard times.

> The jarl bears a heavy burden in a hard time. We all do. What Ravnskjær needs now is no more idle talk, but steadfast remembrance, a steady hand and the will to stand together when winter and darkness test us again.

He ended with words that deserve to endure:

> A settlement that remembers its dead stands stronger among the living.
> Farewell, brother.

There was funeral ale and food, but the gathering was quieter. The unease of the last feast still lingered.

## The trail of silver

A couple of the coins from Blodskjæft were shown to Æthelberth. What the company had taken for foreign, perhaps English markings now acquired another meaning. The monk connected the mark with Alcuin, called “Alcuin the Great”, a scholar serving at Charlemagne's court.

Æthelberth said:

> The silver you found in Blodskjæft is more than foreign. It bears traces of men and powers far south of our fjords. Alcuin is not a warrior's name, but a scholar's — a man at Charlemagne's court. If such coins have found their way here, it means the night creatures you killed did not spring from our soil alone. They have followed paths of silver, books and blood all the way north to Ravnskjær.

He saw more than trade in this. As he understood it, other powers followed in the shadows of the priests, scholars and merchants who travelled out from Charlemagne's world.

> Some seek souls. Some seek silver. Some seek obedience. Where conversion comes as cross and prayer in daylight, the night creatures precede it as cold, hunger and secret bargains. They do not make the land Christian themselves — they make it weak enough for others to shape it.

This was Æthelberth's reading of the connection. The coins gave them a name to follow, but no knowledge of every hand the silver had passed through, or who knew of the creatures in the cave.

## The summer before them

In the time that followed, the six rested and let their wounds heal. They planned to take the box to Jotunheimen later that summer and return before winter set in fully.

Ravnskjær was growing again. Its population was approaching what it had been a couple of years earlier, and hopes of further growth endured. Elrik and his boatbuilders completed a new, larger boat. It still awaited launching and the next voyage west.

When the company had recovered their strength, they set out with the box. Their wounds had healed enough for the journey; they still bore the marks of Blodskjæft. A long road lay ahead. On the first day, they went only a short way beyond Ravnskjær.

## At the forest's edge

What happened there was told to me by those who went. Two bear cubs approached the company. Their mother was nowhere to be seen. They discussed what to do without reaching agreement. Then Bjarte killed one of the cubs.

Asgeir, Saia and Trym made their distress and frustration plain. Jardar and Torstein did not know how to react. The other cub fled.

Then the mother bear charged out of the woods in fury.

Asgeir was struck so hard that he was hurled some fifteen metres backwards. His life hung by a thread. Bjarte and Torstein were also badly wounded. Jardar suffered scratches, while Saia and Trym escaped relatively unharmed.

The bear fought on through wounds that would otherwise have felled beast or person. At last she died defending the cub she had left. It stood for a moment at the edge of the path, watching the company, before disappearing into the forest.

After the fight, they managed to stabilise Asgeir. They could go no farther. The box had to be carried back to Ravnskjær with those who had set out to take it away.

Once again they had to let their bodies heal and discuss the way ahead. Summer still had days to give, but the question now was whether they could reach Jotunheimen before winter drew its veil over the land.
