## The duties are accepted

When the travellers were home again, they accepted the duties Bjørn had proposed before their departure. Jardar was given responsibility for the guards and reserves. Torstein became leader of the hird. Asgeir was to oversee the hunting party, and Trym to lead the rituals and the settlement's spiritual work.

Saia took her place on the small council alongside Åsmund, Ragna, Brynhild and Inga. She was to be the voice of the hird, with an ear for what was happening among the people.

## The wedding after the return

Shortly after the company came home, Torstein married Tilde, the youngest daughter of the late Finn Tjugeskalle. The marriage was intended to strengthen ties between the families and prevent the Tjugeskalle people from turning against the leadership. Two successful westward voyages had not, on their own, put all distrust to rest.

After the wedding, Tilde became pregnant. I add here, with the knowledge of later times, that their child was born in the summer of 794. Throughout the winter, the birth still lay in the future.

## Work through the winter

Autumn gave way to winter, and winter endured. Watch schedules and patrols were established. Jardar was intent on protecting the inhabitants, and Gunhild Treløse was particularly dear to him. He had rescued her from Steinvik many years earlier.

Asgeir and Brynhild organised the hunting parties. When he had the chance, Asgeir also helped the smith. Saia tended her horse and settled into the responsibilities she had been given.

Trym often visited Ben-Gerd and learned what he could from her. He also began approaching Latin alongside Æthelberth, Yrsa and me. Several in the company continued with their English. The words that had first been uncertain sounds between strangers slowly grew easier to use.

Æthelberth was now lodged in the settlement, and Eadgifu lived with Bjarte. The new people and connections became part of daily life while the cold kept us indoors.

## What was said by the hearths

Rumours did not rest for winter. Tor was said to have been seen farther north, in the Skardvik area. No certain explanation for his disappearance accompanied the news. Wolves were seen less often than before, and Vilmar had not been observed nearby.

There was also talk of jötnar still living, and of the four sleepers. An old legend told of four primordial jötnar bound beneath the mountains before humankind's time. If the gate to the realm of the dead were opened, it was said, their dreams might awaken and bring frost, fire, flood and darkness upon the world. Some believed ravens gathered above the places where they slept.

I record this among the things that were said. Repeating a legend on a dark evening does not make every word certain.

And still the earth was miserly. What had been sown after the first voyage had yielded less than hoped. Some wondered whether something was still wrong with the soil. We had no certain answer.
