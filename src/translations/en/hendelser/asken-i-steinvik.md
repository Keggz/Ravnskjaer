## Smoke in the west

The smoke pointed towards Steinvik. Jardar felt called to go there, and the journey was shorter than the road south to Skjeggestad.

Along the way, they found an abandoned house. In the cellar lay shrivelled corpses that had been there for some time. The company moved on; the account offers no certain explanation for the dead.

Steinvik had burned down, just as Jardar remembered it. But someone had cleared the ruins and tended to the dead. The bodies had been buried. When they arrived, there was no one to be seen.

## Gyda's voice

Jardar sensed that Gyda, his deceased wife, was near. Trym opened himself to what was there, and through him Gyda was able to speak.

There were tears among the ruins. Gyda swore that she would watch over Jardar. I have no word-for-word speech to record, but the promise remains in what they told me: the dead woman had not turned her back on him.

## The four riders

The sound of galloping horses broke the silence. Those who arrived were the ulfhednar Skjold-Brynjar, Runa Måneveis, Geir Ulvhjart and Yrsa Nattøyne.

They lit a fire and spent the evening and night in conversation. The company had brought the box. Now they spoke of it, of the keys, and of how important this object was to the task ahead.

The ulfhednar said that the opposition reached beyond what an ordinary person could face with shield and steel. They spoke of werewolves, blood-drinkers and magicians, and of greater powers — gods, jötnar and forces that intervened in the world. The words had to take root in what the company already knew. How much each of them took in that night, I do not know.

I record what was said by the fire without pretending that every question had thereby been answered. The box had been carried up from a burial chamber. Now the burden seemed heavier than before.

## Timber for defence and voyage

Back in Ravnskjær, they reported to Bjørn and Inga. They withheld what they themselves thought unnecessary to tell, and said there was no cause for concern for the time being. Yet they asked for stronger defences.

Orders were given for a palisade and a small ditch outside it, with sharpened stakes to make the approach more difficult. Work began by felling the forest nearest the settlement. It was a major undertaking that would take time.

Elrik was also told to build a new, larger ship, with room for more people and cargo on the next westward voyage. Ravnskjær was preparing both to stand firm and to venture out again.
