## A new man in the hird

After returning home, the travellers needed time to recover. Kjartan Klippen's place had to be filled, and Bjarte Berget was taken into the hird. He had not joined the first voyage to Lindisfarne.

While the settlement regained its strength, Asgeir worked on the idea of a flaming arrow. Jardar had another idea: a helmet with small chambers on top that could be lit to release smoke. Knut En-øye accepted the plans and would work on them further.

## The dark watchpost

One evening, Jardar inspected the defences and watchposts. At one checkpoint, the light was missing. The guard was gone. On the ground, he found blood and signs that something had been dragged away.

He raised the alarm. The settlement's readiness was heightened, while Jardar, Torstein, Trym, Asgeir, Bjarte, Saia and Finn Tjugeskalle followed the tracks into the forest.

There stood a figure beside the dead guard. They came close enough to see something resembling a human with a wolf's head. Then the creature turned and attacked. As it charged, it grew into an enormous wolf-shape. The company managed to bring their shields together; the fear that struck them was greater than ordinary terror, but they stood firm.

## Finn's last battle

The creature went straight for Finn. Its claws tore him open, and his throat was slashed. The others landed blows before Bjarte felled the monster with a strike that severed its head.

Then a human lay before them.

Bjarte tried to stop Finn's bleeding. He had supported Finn in the settlement's disputes and wanted to save him. But the attempt went wrong; trying to seal the wound, he pressed a finger too hard into the torn throat. Finn died. His wounds had already been mortal.

Bjarte earned the name Ulvebane, Wolfbane, for the blow that stopped the creature. The victory and the loss would be remembered together.

## What they carried home

They carried the dead guard and the creature's body back to Ravnskjær. What had attacked them as a wolf could now only be shown as a human corpse. Finn's wounds and their account of the fight were difficult to reconcile with what people could see.

Among those who had been in the forest, several were close to the jarl. Now his political opponent was dead. Suspicion thus found nourishment, even though the company had witnessed what happened. There was unrest and much talk in the dark.

Both Kjartan Klippen and Finn Tjugeskalle received a worthy farewell. Afterwards, the company consulted Bjørn about what to do next. The smoke in the direction of Steinvik demanded investigation. Skjeggestad would have to wait.
