## Those who took another road

After the journey that stopped at the forest's edge, the wounded needed time. About a month passed before new travellers could leave Ravnskjær with the box.

Meanwhile, Saia chose another road. After consulting the company, she took Æthelberth, some volunteers and Hårek Stjernesyn aboard one of the boats. She meant to settle near the monk's home on the Isle of Wight and establish a trading post. When Hårek and the crew later returned with goods, Saia and Æthelberth had stayed behind.

Responsibilities at home were also divided anew. Bjarte took charge of the guards. Jardar assumed command of Ravnskjær's entire defence, including the hird. Torstein devoted himself to his duties as jarl, though he continued training with the warriors.

When the time came, Trym and Asgeir undertook the journey to Jotunheimen. Ulrik the Little and Alrik the Great, the twins from Hrafnvik, accompanied them as protection. Four departed with the box. None of them brought it home.

## A voice for the company

They wanted a skald who could travel with them, speak on their behalf and preserve their deeds. I had asked for more time to study Latin and English. After recording so many dead men, I had lost some of my appetite for seeking out more bloodshed.

Jardar, Torstein and Bjarte sat as judges when four skalds came to try their skill.

Åsleik Nefja was broad-shouldered and in his forties. A scar divided his upper lip, leaving one corner of his mouth trailing when he laughed. He smelled of ale long before the day called for it, sat without invitation and drank from another person's cup while waiting for an objection.

Kadlin Bjørnsdatter was dark-haired, in her thirties and barefoot on the stone floor. She spoke her name quietly once. Then she stood motionless, looking towards the hearth. Once she turned her head towards the doorway. No one was there.

Ingimund Sølvtunge was young, fair-haired and finely dressed, with a silver clasp that did not quite belong with the rest. He was speaking before he was properly inside, laughing at his own words and swiftly finding his way to names and familiarity. He had scarcely met someone before speaking as though they already knew each other.

Torbjørn Kvedulvsson was old and thin, in a worn but well-kept cloak. His hands trembled at rest. He waited rather than greeting anyone, and let a speaker finish completely before answering.

Each performed a poem. The choice fell on Ingimund. They liked his words and saw the worth of a man who could find his footing among strangers. Kadlin and Åsleik eventually moved on. Torbjørn stayed. The next morning they found him blind drunk in the pigsty.

## The rider with the box

Ten nights after Trym's company departed, a horse approached Ravnskjær carrying an unconscious rider. It was Runa Måneveis, the ulfhednar they knew from Steinvik. She was badly wounded.

The guards carried her to Vera and Ragna. Then they saw what she had brought: the box.

Jardar took charge of it, and Vera watched over Runa. Through the night she sweated and trembled. Among the unintelligible words came fragments they could hear: “They are coming.” “Want the box.” “Chaos.” “Blood.”

The next day she was strong enough for Vera to fetch the others. Runa called upon Trym's spirit, and they received an answer.

He spoke of darkness, cold and wetness. Of blood, death and corruption. The company had been well on their way when wolves attacked. They fought back. More wolves arrived, but some fought one another. Then Trym felt something pierce his back. His memory ended there. He could not tell them what had happened to the others before contact broke.

Runa remembered more, but only in fragments. The ulfhednar had followed the travellers in secret. They expected an attack from the Wyrm's servants and meant to intervene. She remembered Trym and Asgeir dying. The twins were unaccounted for; they feared those two had also fallen.

Skjold-Brynjar had recovered the box and brought Runa away from the fighting. He put her on a horse with the box. Her broken memories offered no answer as to what happened to him afterwards.

## The harbour without an escape

Runa had to rest again. The next day they called the clans and the leading townsfolk to council, including Rolf Bakstein and Knut En-øye. Runa warned that the enemy might be closer than they thought.

Jardar spoke with Rolf. The baker was ready to kill whatever came, with an eagerness Jardar found remarkable. The talkative man at the stone oven seemed to be awaiting something about which he had few words.

Bjarte and Jardar revised the watch. While Bjarte went to check that the orders were being followed, he heard noises from the harbour. He glimpsed bodies, feet by a boathouse and movement near an overturning barrel. Fear caught him for a moment. Then he took his axe and ran.

When he rounded the corner, the attackers were gone. Geir Fieri and another guard lay dead. The boats had been wrecked so that no one could escape by sea.

Bjarte ran back to the council at Vera's. Now they expected the attack that day or evening.

Njoldr, recently promoted to captain of the guard, took command of the troops on the palisade at Jardar's order. The civilians were told to gather food and whatever weapons they had and seek shelter in the longhouse. A few hours later the warning came: the enemy stood at the forest's edge.

## The deadline at sunset

One man crossed the open ground. Torstein, Jardar and Bjarte went out through the gate to meet him. Above them, the archers stood ready.

He demanded the box. Surrender it, and they would be allowed to escape. He also said he remembered what they had done to his brother a couple of years earlier: the wolf-creature Bjarte had slain near Ravnskjær. None could know whether that kinship was true.

Their answer was that he would have to come and take what he wanted. Ravnskjær would not yield so easily. He gave them until sunset and returned towards the woods.

The defenders waited on the palisade. Hundreds of eyes glittered between the trees. Some figures looked human, others animal. None advanced.

Runa was on her feet again. She looked across to the other side and said she must defend Ravnskjær there for as long as she could. Shortly afterwards she vanished from their sight.

Light rain began to fall. As daylight faded, the envoy stepped onto the plain again.

“So … what will it be?”

The answer came as a sea of arrows.

Torstein called on everyone to fight for Ravnskjær and their own people, for everything they had to live for. If they fell, Valhalla's golden halls awaited. His words gave them courage to stand.

Rolf asked for the gate to be opened. He was ready.

## What came out of the forest

They heard the feet before they could distinguish all the bodies. Corrupted people and animals streamed towards them. Six figures remained farther back.

Among the first came creatures that still looked like men at a distance. Then their forearms split and bone blades unfolded. Others carried too many limbs hidden beneath human skin. Dogs and wolf-like beasts had double jaws and ridges of bone where their fur was missing. A bear's body was rotting even as it ran.

Behind them screamed an almost naked figure with a distended chest. The sound seemed to rise from the very cavity beneath its ribs. Another carried an enormous belly and a jaw that opened towards its chest. Others had frost on blue skin or backs full of breathing holes, with something crawling beneath the hide.

Later, they learned a name for such creatures: fomori. That evening, the guards first saw all the things a living being should never become.

Rolf ran straight at them and struck down two of the foremost. The men on the palisade stared. Then they decided: he would not stand there alone.

Jardar and Njoldr descended nimbly. Bjarte and Torstein followed with less grace and just as much resolve. The guards poured through the gate to meet the assault.

## The gate that broke

This was no even line of shield against shield. Bone blades found gaps between the rims. A guard was dragged out of formation by wet limbs unfolding from an attacker's sides. Another fell beneath the hound bodies and did not rise. When the scream from the swollen chest rose, men who had stood firm faltered; the enemy took the ground they yielded.

The enormous maw closed upon one of the fallen. Swords could stop many things. They could not stop everything at once.

Still, the defenders held together where they could. Jardar's spear met creatures before they reached the shields. Torstein's swords worked through the openings, and Bjarte hewed space whenever the line threatened to be overrun. Around them, guards closed ranks behind those who fell. New hands lifted spears. A name was called with no reply, but the shield beside it remained.

Then one of the largest reached the gate. Nearly three metres tall, swollen with knots of hardened tissue, it looked assembled from flesh rather than born. A “Husk”, as the creature was later called.

Bjarte went against it. His axe bit into the overgrown body while it hammered the timber. The gate gave way before he could bring the monster down. When it finally lay still, the opening it had made remained.

The defenders grew fewer. At last they had to withdraw inside. There they gathered their shields again and backed away in formation. Rolf was out of sight.

## Three wolves within

Njoldr and several guards were still with them. Torstein, Jardar and Bjarte stood at the front with Ulf Blodøye and Harald Hårmagre.

The enemy followed slowly. Then they stopped in a semicircle and watched the defenders as though awaiting a signal.

An enormous werewolf leapt over the palisade. Two others followed. Their bodies towered over humans, upright in war-form, with claws and jaws that made the shields look small. Through the fur the defenders glimpsed distorted backs, overlong arms and black veins in bare skin.

Roughly half the shield line broke in terror. Weapons and shields fell, and men ran towards the longhouse. Two wolves pursued them. The largest remained before those who held their ground and extended a hand. It wanted the box.

They answered by beating their shields.

Jardar and Bjarte broke away in opposite directions after the wolves chasing the fugitives. The creatures overtook people along the way. Beyond them stood the longhouse, full of those the defenders had sworn to protect.

The largest hurled itself at the line. Claws drove beneath Ulf Blodøye's chin and through his head. Harald Hårmagre's belly was torn open. The last guards fled. Torstein remained.

He landed his blows. One sword severed an arm from the monster. The other he drove up through its throat. The huge body collapsed before the jarl.

Bjarte caught his opponent and seized its jaw. With a strength no one hearing the account could readily explain, he tore the lower jaw away. The creature fell. Jardar pinned the third against the longhouse wall with his spear, held it there and killed it.

They had slain three. They had not seen the last three coming behind them.

## When the box opened

Arle came running out of the longhouse.

“I'll help you, Jardar!”

The boy struck at the werewolf nearest him. It answered with a backhand that hurled him hard against the longhouse.

Then a claw pierced Jardar's belly. Half of Bjarte's throat was torn open. Torstein's abdomen and torso were ripped apart. They lay at death's door with nothing left to offer in resistance.

One werewolf took the box. It placed keys in the lock. None of the three knew how the enemy had obtained them. Their eyelids grew heavy, and sleep crept over them as the lid opened.

Everything went dark.

Then something woke within them.

What follows I can preserve only as they themselves tried to explain it. No one standing outside their bodies could have seen everything they felt.

## The blood awoke

For Jardar it began in his teeth, as though something were pushing them out from within. His ribcage cracked. Sounds grew sharper than he could bear: crying, heartbeats, failing breath. The smell of blood and fear filled him, and behind it he sensed the enemy as he had never sensed anything before.

His back twisted. His fingers spread, and dark claws broke through. Fur forced its way through his skin. Pain gave way to a rage older than thought. When he rose, he was nearly three metres tall. The howl he heard came from his own throat. Jardar had awakened as Garou.

Torstein felt cold. Beneath it came a thud from the earth, as though something vast slept under the world and his heart answered it. His hands broadened. Claws pushed through. His shoulders grew, and his body's weight exceeded anything he had known.

From the ground beneath his knees came one word: Stand.

He set his hand upon the earth and rose. Dark fur faded into grey and white, with frost among the hairs. The bear carried more than fury. It carried the certainty that he could take another blow and remain between the enemy and the others. Torstein awakened as Gurahl.

Bjarte saw the box standing open. Behind the houses, another forest suddenly overlaid the real one. Trees passed through walls. Roots reached through the dead without moving them. Above Ravnskjær stood a presence so vast that its head vanished into darkness. It changed whenever he tried to look: a wolf's head, a bear's shoulders, something he could not name.

He felt as though that presence had watched him all his life.

Something travelled from the earth into his blood and gripped his heart. Bjarte felt the weight of the dead and wounded around him, and the emptiness when a breath ceased. He refused to remain fallen. His fingers dug into the ground, his arms grew, and his chest expanded. When he rose, the bear came with him. Bjarte, too, was Gurahl.

## Those who turned the hunt

The werewolves had time to see it. For a moment, they were the ones who hesitated.

Then the three were upon them. Claws tore where human weapons had scarcely been enough. Bodies were broken apart, and the enemies who had stood over dying men were slain by them.

Fury drove Jardar, Torstein and Bjarte onwards against the fomori now trying to flee. Outside the palisade, the fugitives met another figure: a huge boar-being that struck them down and drove them back. Together, the defenders drove the remaining creatures away.

The boar proved to be Rolf Bakstein.

After a brief exchange, their bodies began to change back. Soon the four stood as humans again, without the clothes their transformations had destroyed.

Then a splash came from the water by the longhouse. Hårek Stjernesyn emerged, also restored to human shape after a different transformation: that of a shark. He could only say that he had felt compelled to go swimming. He did not yet understand what had happened.

Here I end this account. The enemy had been driven away. What the night had taken from Ravnskjær, and what it had awakened, the living would need time to understand.
