## The scout's warning

Before the scout arrived, Bjarte had already felt something in his body. He understood what it heralded: Eldrbrand was on his way.

In the late summer of 794, a scout came riding at a furious pace. An enormous creature was approaching Ravnskjær. It was only a few days' journey away.

Bjørn gathered the company and Ben-Gerd for counsel. Eldrbrand was coming, the jotunn who had already laid Steinvik waste. Ben-Gerd believed he had to be fought on two sides of the world at once: here, where his body could crush houses and men, and where his essence dwelt.

She called that place Muspelheim. She could not explain precisely where she would send them, but was certain they would arrive. The way lay through a drink that would put them into a trance. The brew was poisonous. Without the antidote she had prepared, they might die of the journey before returning to their own bodies.

The evacuation began that same day. People left by boat and on foot to get far enough away. The hird, guards, reserves, Bjørn, Eirik Ravnblod and the six who had faced so many of Ravnskjær's dangers remained.

I was not among those who stayed for the battle. What follows I have gathered from the survivors. They witnessed different parts of the same struggle, and none could see it all. Where memory fails, my pen must not pretend to know.

## Three here and three beyond

Jardar, Trym and Bjarte took Ben-Gerd's drink when they heard Eldrbrand approaching. Asgeir, Saia and Torstein remained with the defenders. Their plan was to use horses to delay the jotunn and draw him away from Ravnskjær for as long as they could.

Those who saw him coming spoke of smoke, fire and ash above the mountains. Eldrbrand was said to stand some thirty metres tall. Men who had fought before froze at the sight. Bjørn rallied them.

They could exchange words with the jotunn. They could not talk him out of attacking. They had expected such a vast body to move slowly. That mistake nearly cost Saia her life; only the riding skill she had worked to acquire got her clear.

Eldrbrand hurled a great stone at Ravnskjær. It did not strike as well as the next would, but houses were smashed and a few men killed. The battle for the settlement had begun.

## The tree and the nine doors

The three who drank said they awoke in a dark chamber. They could make out an enormous tree, with an opening at its root. Everything around them seemed strangely insubstantial. They went beneath the tree and entered a chamber with nine doors.

The doors are remembered thus:

1. **Cold without frost.** The air stood still, as though something were absent. It was associated with Niflheim and profound cold.
2. **Silence with weight.** A door that weighed on the mind more than the body: Hel, the realm of the dead.
3. **Heat and ash.** Black, cracked stone surrounded the way to what Ben-Gerd had called Muspelheim.
4. **Mist and echoes.** A door the eye struggled to focus on. The name Nivlheim was used for this in-between state.
5. **Earth and roots.** The smell of wet earth and metal, associated with Svartálfaheim and the powers beneath the ground.
6. **Wind and pursuit.** A restless threshold, linked to Vanaheim and untamed nature.
7. **Shadows with a pulse.** Neither old nor new, but strangely close: wolves, the hunt and a veil.
8. **Light that stings.** An uncomfortable light, associated with Asgard and the higher powers.
9. **Silence that looks back.** A sealed, unknown door that gave off no feeling at all.

These are names and impressions passed on to me, not a complete chart of the world. Bjarte felt drawn to the third door, towards the fire and something that answered the jotunn blood within him. Jardar, for his part, felt drawn towards the seventh door. Even so, they chose the way of fire. Bjarte went first. Jardar and Trym followed.

None of the other doors was tried.

## The road through fire

Beyond the door lay lava, hard ground, ash and heat that filled everything. A long paved road stretched as far as they could see. They followed it inward, surrounded by a sea of lava.

Several times the ground shook violently. When the accounts were later set beside one another, the first tremor coincided with Eldrbrand's name being spoken on the other side of their journey, back among the defenders.

On the rocks and small patches of land, more and more little beings of fire, ash and lava gathered. None of the three has given me a name for them. They stayed beside the road as the company continued.

At last the landscape opened around a great flame. Three small altar towers stood in a triangle around it. Farther in stood a seat like a throne, with a waterfall of lava behind it.

Each traveller had to face an altar of his own. Jardar surrendered something personal connected to Gyda; what it was has not been recorded. Bjarte let his anger and jotunn blood rise and smashed his tower. Trym's trial called upon the occult knowledge he had sought for so long. In this way they overcame all three.

## Ben-Gerd's last protection

While the three approached the fire, the others kept the jotunn occupied at Ravnskjær. Asgeir displayed the box to Eldrbrand. The jotunn charged after him, far faster than Asgeir had hoped.

Ben-Gerd managed to throw Asgeir clear. The hand meant to seize him closed around her instead.

Those close enough thought Eldrbrand looked pleased. He and Ben-Gerd seemed to exchange a few words. No one has told me what was said. Then he crushed her in his hand and continued.

Torstein searched for a way to lure the jotunn into the water. He found none.

When the men saw Ben-Gerd fall, Bjørn led them forward. His words have been passed on as follows:

> Today Ravnskjær will not fall; we endure! May Odin be with us! TO VALHALLA!

The jarl ordered the attack. Eldrbrand answered with a boulder.

The survivors later estimated that roughly three out of four guards were killed by the throw. The stone tore on through the settlement, coming to rest only after destroying large parts of the buildings and half the longhouse. A whole defence could vanish beneath one stone. Yet the battle was not decided.

## The cold within the flame

At the altar towers, the flame grew. A figure emerged: Eldrbrand, as the three encountered his essence. According to the accounts they later shared, this happened at about the moment the jotunn at home paused and turned towards Asgeir.

A few words passed before the fighting began. Jardar, Trym and Bjarte faced him on the other side while Ravnskjær was being crushed around the bodies they had left behind.

Trym opened himself and called upon Náðskrímr. The frost spirit lent him strength for a time, but the cold demanded more than he could bear. It wanted to take possession of him.

He managed to drive it out. Doing so nearly cost him his life. Jardar and Bjarte were also badly wounded before the three finally defeated Eldrbrand's essence.

Trym survived this battle. That must be written plainly, even though his name would later be entered among the dead.

## The sea rises

Outside Ravnskjær, the defenders noticed that Eldrbrand no longer seemed so certain. Then came the crash.

Part of the sea rose and swept over the settlement. The water gathered into a tremendous jet that struck the jotunn. This was Vilmar's work. The wanderer intervened with a power they had never seen him use before, and paid dearly for it.

Eldrbrand fell onto his back. A distinct opening appeared in his chest.

Torstein rode alongside and tried to leap onto the jotunn at speed. He reached him, but could not hold on. The fall left him badly hurt.

Saia attempted the same approach. She found her grip, climbed onto his chest and drove her sword as far into the opening as she could.

On the other side, the fire beings grew restless. The ground shook, the lava falls swelled, and the sky itself seemed about to collapse. Jardar, Trym and Bjarte fled back along the road. Looking towards the falls one last time, they thought they glimpsed a vast creature coming towards them. They believed it might be Surtr. I have no greater certainty than their words.

Then they were back in Ben-Gerd's hut.

## Those who returned

Ben-Gerd was gone. Eadgifu sat with the three and gave them the antidote that carried them through the danger of the brew. They did not yet know Eldrbrand had been defeated.

Saia had climbed down. The jotunn's body vanished, but the ground where he had lain remained black, ruined and hot. It was still so when this account was written.

Jardar, Bjarte and Trym came out onto the battlefield and found Asgeir, Saia and Torstein alive. For a moment they could breathe. Then they had to see what victory had cost.

Asgeir was relatively unhurt and took it upon himself to search the settlement. Where the stones had struck, the earth had been torn open. There were trails of blood with no whole bodies at their ends.

Bjørn lay inside the gate. The boulder had destroyed the lower part of his body, but he was still breathing. When Asgeir reached him, the jarl met him with a questioning look. Asgeir nodded. Bjørn drew his last breath.

Asgeir counted only about fifteen survivors among those who had remained at Ravnskjær, many of them injured. That number concerns those he found after the battle, not the people sent away before the jotunn came. Roughly half the settlement had been destroyed.

Vilmar appeared again. He made less of his presence than usual and was visibly affected by what he had done. He had saved them, but had not come through unharmed himself.

## Before we could rebuild

They sent word to Skjeggestad that it was safe to return. While waiting for the people, they did what they could to clear the wreckage and honour the dead.

In the days that followed, the company questioned Vilmar about the world they had glimpsed. Their conversations continued over several days. The wanderer belonged to an order called Askebærerne. More of what he told them must wait until it can be properly recorded.

Ben-Gerd did not return. Bjørn did not return. Ravnskjær stood, but no one who saw it afterwards could believe it had endured unchanged.

I shall write later of the funeral and the work of raising the settlement again. Here ends the account of the day we had to fight on both sides of the world to keep our own.
