## The wanderer behind the bush

Several strangers had come to Ravnskjær. One had drawn particular attention because no one knew of any previous connection between him and the settlement.

Saia met him on her way to Ben-Gerd. He emerged from behind a bush and introduced himself as Vilmar Vandreren. Later, the company also called him the bush man.

Vilmar wanted to know whether they had the box. They must take good care of it, he said. He had been watching them, and their paths would cross again. Saia understood little of the stranger's purpose and found the encounter unsettling, but carried his words to the others.

Later, they visited Ben-Gerd. New visions showed standing stones arranged in circles far to the west. Trym felt a particularly strong pull towards them. They assumed the vision had something to do with the box, though the reason remained unknown.

## Hjalti's news from the south

The nearest threat nevertheless lay to the south. Hjalti Urdsson returned with a troubling report from Skjeggestad. Trade continued there, but the town had changed: fewer people in the streets, closed doors and people glancing over their shoulders before speaking.

He had heard of disappearances. A sailor said both his brother and his brother's wife had vanished, and that only the woman's clothes remained. Ola Sjørev was also losing men. There was activity at the river mouth, with tents, wagons and guards, but the force seemed smaller than before, and Ola was said to be nervous.

Hjalti believed something was being planned against Ravnskjær, but that an attack would have to wait while they tried to understand what was taking their people. He could not say for certain what the darkness in Skjeggestad was.

After consulting Bjørn, the company chose to go there posing as mercenaries. They gathered horses and equipment. Jardar and Asgeir also collected what Knut En-øye had made from their plans. Early the next morning, Jardar, Torstein, Trym, Asgeir and Saia rode south. Bjarte remained in Ravnskjær.

## The woman on the path

The journey took about four days. On the third day, during a rest near the forest, they heard someone approaching. A young woman ran out between the trees, frightened and pursued.

She said her family had been killed and at least five men were after her. She had lived in the forest not far from Skjeggestad. The company gave her one of the horses and sent her towards Ravnskjær, telling her to mention their names. Whether she arrived is not recorded here.

They themselves stayed and waited. When the pursuers arrived, a brief exchange of words was followed by fighting. The company struck them down without suffering wounds themselves. Asgeir felled one man with an arrow to the jaw. The bandits' leader was taken alive and kept alive long enough to be questioned.

Jardar and Torstein used cruel means. The man said many of Ola's warriors had died under strange circumstances: they were unusually pale and had marks on their necks. An attack on Ravnskjær thus seemed less imminent than the company had believed.

He also told them about the town, the palisade and the training ground outside, where Ola and his remaining warriors often gathered. After the questioning, they killed him and continued their journey.

## Entering as mercenaries

From a ridge, they looked out over Skjeggestad. A palisade with watchtowers and one main entrance surrounded the town. One of the towers on the far side appeared unmanned. The company moved closer under cover and found rotten beams that could be broken open.

Then they approached the gate and offered their services as mercenaries. They had heard the town was short of warriors. The guards sent them to the jarl's house, farthest from the entrance. They then saw that the weak beams lay behind the house.

Inside the town were houses and tents, adults and children, and a moderate guard force. Barrels stood in several places. The company examined them and built a plan around what they could use for celebration and fire.

The jarl welcomed them. They convinced him that they would fight for Skjeggestad against Ravnskjær, and were told to report to Ola when he returned from the training ground at dusk. Until then, they could move freely through the town.

## The games in Skjeggestad

Jardar and Torstein gathered the inhabitants for festivities and barrel-rolling. While people were occupied with the games, flammable contents were spread from the barrels.

At the same time, Trym, Asgeir and Saia took valuables from the storehouse beside the jarl's house. They carried the loot through the opening by the rotten beams and out to a hiding place in a copse. There they gathered the horses, mead and treasures.

The inhabitants had welcomed strangers who said they would protect the town. Now they played while an escape route and the plunder were prepared behind them.

## When the gate fell

Before dusk, the guards shouted that Ola and his men were approaching. The company set fire to what had been soaked in flammable material. Flames spread rapidly through Skjeggestad.

The guards tried to save people, but the gate collapsed. The inhabitants were trapped inside. The children and adults whom the company had seen between the houses and tents were still there when the town burned.

Outside, the company took cover and waited for Ola and his warriors, who came running towards the fire. Some words were exchanged, but they have not been preserved in this account. The battle that followed left the travellers with injuries and scars. Ola and his men fell one by one, while the screams from the town continued behind them.

## The silence afterwards

They gathered by the plunder in the woods and waited until the town had fallen silent and the fire had done its work. When they looked into the ruins, they found no sign of survivors and concluded that no one had escaped.

Then they returned to Ravnskjær with more treasures. The threat from Skjeggestad had been dealt with, it was said. I also record what accompanied those words: the town and the people who lived there were gone.

## The report in the longhouse

At home, they quickly called Bjørn to the longhouse. Skjeggestad was no longer a threat, they said. The town had burned down, and no one had survived.

The jarl questioned this. Their answers were evasive. In time, the weight of what had been done also began to reach several of them. There had been more than warriors inside the palisade. They had seen the people there before the fire took them.

Now they were to discuss the way ahead. But what had happened in the south could not be closed as easily as a report.
