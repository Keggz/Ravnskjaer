Æthelberth was found as the sole survivor of a shipwreck during the second westward voyage in 793. The company called him Erte-Bert and described him as a good-natured man.

## From shipwreck to Ravnskjær

He was first put ashore at his home on the Isle of Wight. Curious about the company and their way of life, he asked them to collect him on their return journey. Saia came back for him, and he willingly accompanied them to Ravnskjær, where he was given lodging.

He and Saia formed a close friendship. During the winter he was among those with whom Trym learned a little Latin, alongside Halvdan Skrivare and Yrsa Åsmundsdatter.

## The coins from Blodskjæft

After the journey to Blodskjæft in the summer of 795, Æthelberth interpreted the mark on a couple of the coins the company had found. He linked them to Alcuin, called “Alcuin the Great”, a scholar at Charlemagne's court. He read the silver as a trace of connections between the night creatures and powers far to the south, warning that such beings could weaken communities before Christian conversion reached them. This was his explanation of those connections, not evidence that Alcuin or Charlemagne himself commanded the creatures.

## Homeward with Saia

Later in 795, Æthelberth returned to his home on the Isle of Wight with Saia, Hårek Stjernesyn and volunteers. Saia intended to live there and establish a trading post. Æthelberth and Saia stayed when Hårek and the crew returned to Ravnskjær with goods.
