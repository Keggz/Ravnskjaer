Øystæinn «Sølvskeptikeren» Spjótaldr was Åsmund Helgason's younger brother and belonged to the Helga clan. He is described as sceptical and keenly concerned with finances.

Øystæinn is dead.

## Returning in the autumn of 794

Early in the autumn after Eldrbrand's attack, Øystæinn returned to Ravnskjær after several years away. He was clearly sceptical about how the settlement would be governed. Vali, Sjur, Olve and Einar liked him for his views.

This was before his death during the journey to Blodskjæft in early summer 795.

## The trade and Blodskjæft

During the winter of 794–795, rumours circulated about Øystæinn's trading, and he grew thinner and more sickly. During the feast in early summer 795, Asgeir followed him to a meeting in the forest and afterwards found blood in urns among the trade goods.

Torstein accused him publicly, later struck him unconscious and had him bound in Bjarte's house. When Bjarte offered him something to drink, Øystæinn said that creatures in Blodskjæft by Myrkagap forced him to obtain goods and information by threatening those close to him.

From the marks on his neck and Parchment Scroll III, Trym believed he had been made a “spawn”, a bound servant. This was a deduction, not a complete explanation of his condition. Øystæinn was to help the company and guide them to the cave. He was alive during the feast and subsequently accompanied the expedition.

## Death in the cave

Øystæinn guided the company to Blodskjæft. He knew the trading operation, but had never gone beyond the first chamber. During the attack he was at the rear near the altar chamber, behind Asgeir, Saia and Trym.

He tried to get away during the battle and was killed. The notes do not establish who killed him or exactly how. The other six survived and ensured his body was brought back to Ravnskjær with dignity.

## The funeral

After his return, Øystæinn received a funeral worthy of his standing. Åsmund spoke before his brother's final journey out to sea, urging Ravnskjær to remember its dead and stand together. Funeral ale and a quieter gathering followed, while the unease after the spring feast still lingered.
